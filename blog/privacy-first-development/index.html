Privacy-First Android Apps: GDPR and Google Play Guide

AppMaven Studio
Independent Android developer
Dec 28, 2025
6 min read

Privacy on Android is no longer only a legal question. Google Play asks every developer to describe their data handling in the Data safety form, requires account deletion in apps that have accounts, and requires a certified consent tool for ads shown in Europe. This guide covers the design principles, what the GDPR actually says about consent and user rights, and the Android and Google Play requirements to meet in 2026.

Privacy by Design Starts With Less Data

Article 25 of the GDPR is titled "Data protection by design and by default". It asks controllers to make sure that, by default, only the personal data necessary for each specific purpose is processed, which covers how much you collect, how far you process it and how long you keep it. That translates into a few habits:

  • Data minimisation: for every field and every SDK, ask whether the feature works without it. Data you never collect cannot leak, does not need a deletion process and does not appear in your Data safety form.
  • Purpose limitation: use data only for the purpose you told users about.
  • Storage limitation: decide how long each type of data is kept and delete it on a schedule.
  • On-device first: if a calculation can run on the phone, the data does not need to leave it.
  • Privacy-friendly defaults: optional sharing should start switched off.

What the GDPR Requires, Precisely

The GDPR can apply to a developer outside the EU when the app is offered to people in the Union or monitors their behaviour there; recitals 23 and 24 explain how that is judged. A few points are often oversimplified:

  • Lawful basis: consent is one basis among several, alongside contract, legal obligation and legitimate interests. Pick the right one for each purpose and document it.
  • Consent: it needs a clear affirmative act. Silence, pre-ticked boxes or inactivity are not consent (recital 32), and under Article 7(3) it must be as easy to withdraw consent as to give it.
  • Response time: requests from users must be answered without undue delay and within one month, which can be extended by two further months for complex or numerous requests (Article 12(3)).
  • Erasure is not unconditional: Article 17 gives a right to erasure on specific grounds, such as the data no longer being necessary or consent being withdrawn with no other legal ground. It does not apply where processing is needed, for example, to comply with a legal obligation or to establish, exercise or defend legal claims.
  • Portability is narrower than it sounds: Article 20 applies only when processing is based on consent or a contract and is carried out by automated means. In that case users can receive the data they provided in a structured, commonly used and machine-readable format.
  • Penalties: the highest tier of fines reaches 20 million euros or 4% of total worldwide annual turnover, whichever is higher (Article 83(5)).

Following the GDPR does not automatically make an app compliant everywhere. Other countries and US states have their own definitions, deadlines and opt-out rules, so check the laws of each market you target.

Google Play Requirements You Cannot Skip

Data Safety Section

Every app on Google Play must complete the Data safety form, including apps that collect no user data, and every app needs a privacy policy. The form covers data handled by the SDKs and libraries inside your app, not only your own code. "Collection" means transmitting data off the device; "sharing" means transferring it to a third party, with service providers that process data on your behalf generally excluded. Before you fill it in, list every SDK in the final build and read each vendor's data disclosure.

Account Deletion

If users can create an account from within your app, Google Play's account deletion requirements apply. You must offer an in-app path to delete the account and its data, and a web link resource where people can request deletion of the account and its data. Data you declared as collected in the Data safety section is in scope. You may keep some data for reasons such as security, fraud prevention or regulatory compliance, but you have to tell users, for example in your privacy policy.

Families Policy

If children are one of your target audiences, the Families policy adds stricter rules. Apps that solely target children must not send the advertising ID or hardware identifiers, and may not request location permission. Ads shown to children or users of unknown age must come from Google Play Families Self-Certified Ads SDKs, without interest-based advertising or remarketing. The app and its SDKs must also comply with laws such as COPPA and the GDPR.

Consent for Ads in Europe

Google requires publishers to use a Google-certified consent management platform integrated with the IAB Transparency and Consent Framework when serving ads to users in the EEA and the UK (since 16 January 2024) and in Switzerland (since 31 July 2024). Google's own Privacy & messaging European regulations messages are certified, and on Android they are shown through the User Messaging Platform (UMP) SDK. A typical flow:

  • Call requestConsentInfoUpdate() at every app launch.
  • Call loadAndShowConsentFormIfRequired() once the consent status is known.
  • Check canRequestAds() before requesting any ad.
  • If getPrivacyOptionsRequirementStatus() returns REQUIRED, add a visible settings entry that calls showPrivacyOptionsForm(), so users can change their choice at any time.

Our own apps that show ads ask for consent in the EEA and UK through Google's UMP.

Ask for Less on the Device

Every runtime permission is a question users have to answer and a line in your Data safety form. Before adding one, look for an API that does the job without it.

  • Photos and videos: the photo picker gives your app access only to the photos and videos the user selects, instead of the whole media library. Google Play's photo and video permissions policy limits READ_MEDIA_IMAGES and READ_MEDIA_VIDEO to apps whose core functionality needs broad access.
  • Location: declare ACCESS_COARSE_LOCATION and add ACCESS_FINE_LOCATION only if a feature truly benefits from precision. Approximate location is accurate to about 3 square kilometres, and users can choose it even when you ask for precise location, so your app should still work with it. Background location requires its own permission and is restricted by Play's location policy. Apps targeting Android 17 (API level 37) whose features only need location for a single session must use the location button, according to the location permissions guide.
  • Check your SDKs: libraries can add permissions to the merged manifest. Review it before every release.

Store Sensitive Data Safely

Many older guides recommend EncryptedSharedPreferences and EncryptedFile from androidx.security:security-crypto. That advice is outdated: the release notes show that all of the library's APIs were deprecated in version 1.1.0 in favour of existing platform APIs and direct use of the Android Keystore.

  • Keep keys in the Keystore: the Android Keystore keeps key material out of your app process and, where the hardware supports it, inside a Trusted Execution Environment or StrongBox secure element.
  • Restrict key use: you can limit a key to specific algorithms and purposes, or require recent user authentication before it can be used.
  • Mind the main thread: cryptographic operations can be slow, so run them off the UI thread.
  • Encrypt in transit: send personal data only over HTTPS, and keep it out of logs and crash reports.

Be Transparent and Easy to Reach

Write the privacy policy in plain language: what you collect, why, which SDKs receive it, how long you keep it and how people can contact you or delete their data. Keep it consistent with your Data safety form. At AppMaven Studio, each app has its own privacy policy and terms alongside our studio-wide privacy policy, and anyone can ask for their data to be deleted through our data deletion page; we handle those requests within 30 days.

Key Takeaways

  • Collect only what each feature needs; it shrinks your Data safety form and your risk
  • GDPR rights have conditions: erasure has exceptions, portability covers consent or contract data
  • Every Play app needs a Data safety form and a privacy policy, including SDK data
  • Apps with accounts must offer in-app and web account deletion
  • Ads in the EEA, UK and Switzerland need a Google-certified CMP such as the UMP SDK
  • Prefer the photo picker and approximate location over broad permissions
  • Replace deprecated security-crypto with platform APIs and the Android Keystore
