Android App Monetization in 2026: Ads, IAP and Subscriptions

AppMaven Studio
Independent Android developer
Dec 20, 2025
9 min read

How an app makes money shapes how it feels as much as any screen does. This guide covers the main ways to earn from an Android app on Google Play in 2026: ads, one-time purchases and subscriptions, together with the Google Play and AdMob rules that decide what you are allowed to do. Policies change often, so every section links to the official page. Check those pages again before each release.

For context: at AppMaven Studio, some of our free apps and games show ads, and some sell in-app purchases or subscriptions through Google Play Billing. Our apps that show ads ask for consent in the EEA and UK through Google's User Messaging Platform (UMP). Everything else here is general guidance, not data from our apps.

Choose a Model That Fits How People Use the App

  • Ads only: common for free utilities and casual games. Revenue grows with usage, so ads must not drive people away.
  • One-time purchase: unlock a pro feature set or remove ads with a single payment. Simple to explain, and a good fit for tools without ongoing costs.
  • Subscription: fits apps that deliver ongoing value or carry ongoing costs, such as regular content updates, cloud sync or server processing.
  • Hybrid: free with ads plus a paid upgrade, for example an ad-free version sold as an in-app purchase.

A useful test: write one sentence that says what the user gets for the money. If you cannot describe ongoing value, a subscription will feel like a toll.

Google Play's Payments Policy: What Must Use Play Billing

Google Play's Payments policy decides how you can charge. Paid app downloads must use Google Play's billing system, and so must digital items sold inside the app. The policy's examples include virtual currencies, subscriptions, app functionality, cloud services such as data storage, and an ad-free version of an app.

Play Billing is not required for physical goods and physical services (for example transportation, food delivery or tickets for live events), credit card and utility bill payments, peer-to-peer payments, online auctions and tax-exempt donations.

  • No steering. Apps may not lead users to a payment method other than Google Play's billing system for these digital items, whether through the store listing, in-app promotions, web views or buttons. The exceptions are alternative billing and external payment programs that developers must enroll in, and they are only available in eligible countries or regions.
  • Virtual currency bought in an app or game may only be used in that title.
  • Prices must match. In-app pricing must match what the Play billing interface shows, so read prices from Google Play at runtime instead of hard-coding them.
  • Randomized items such as loot boxes must disclose the odds before purchase.
  • Clear terms. Users must be told clearly about the terms and pricing of subscriptions and in-app features.

Keep the Play Billing Library Up to Date

Every version of the Google Play Billing Library has a two-year deprecation cycle. According to the deprecation FAQ, the deadline for new apps and updates was August 31, 2026 for version 7 (with extensions until November 1, 2026), and it is August 31, 2027 for version 8 and August 31, 2028 for version 9. In practice, new apps and updates now need version 8 or newer, and starting a new integration on the latest version gives you the longest runway.

Play Console shows a warning when a release uses an unsupported version. Plan the upgrade into a normal update well before the deadline, because billing code needs testing with real purchase flows.

Subscriptions: Base Plans and Offers

In Play Console you create subscriptions in three layers, in this order: the subscription, its base plans, and optional offers. A subscription is only available to users once at least one base plan is active.

  • Base plans can be auto-renewing, prepaid (the user pays manually to extend), or installments (a fixed monthly amount over a commitment period, available only in some countries and regions).
  • Offers are always linked to one base plan. Eligibility can be new customer acquisition, upgrade from a specific plan, or developer determined, where your app decides who qualifies.
  • Pricing phases within an offer can be a free trial, a single discounted payment, or a discounted recurring payment for a set number of periods.

There is no universal best trial length or price. What does work everywhere is a clear paywall: list what the subscription includes, show the price and billing period exactly as Google Play returns them, state when a free trial ends and charging starts, and tell users where to manage or cancel.

Ads With AdMob: Choose Formats That Respect the Flow

AdMob's ad formats each fit a different moment in an app:

  • Banner: a basic format at the top or bottom of the screen.
  • Interstitial: full-page ads at natural breaks and transitions, such as completing a level.
  • Rewarded: users choose to watch a short video or interact with a playable ad or survey in exchange for a reward.
  • Rewarded interstitial: a full-page format that rewards users for viewing ads during natural breaks.
  • Native: customizable ads that match the look and feel of your app.
  • App open: shown when users open or switch back to your app.

Interstitials: Timing Is Everything

AdMob lists disallowed interstitial implementations. Do not show interstitials when the app loads or exits. Do not show more than one interstitial per two user actions, counting Back button presses, and never one right after the user closes another. Do not launch them while someone is focused on a task such as playing, filling in a form or reading.

The recommended implementations are the mirror image: place interstitials between levels or stages in games and after several screens or actions in other apps, show the ad before the Next or Continue button of a break page rather than after it, add a short delay such as a loading screen if the app encourages fast tapping, and pre-load ads so they appear without delay.

Rewarded Ads: Opt-In and Honest

Under AdMob's policies for ad units that offer rewards, rewarded ads may only be served after the user clearly opts in. Rewarded interstitials need an introductory screen with a working "no" option. Before each rewarded ad you must clearly disclose what the user has to do and what they get, and direct monetary items may never be offered as rewards.

Consent in the EEA, UK and Switzerland

Google's EU user consent policy covers end users in the European Economic Area, the UK and Switzerland. You must obtain legally valid consent for cookies or other local storage where legally required, and for collecting, sharing and using personal data to personalize ads. You also have to identify each party that receives the data, keep records of consent and explain how users can withdraw it.

Google requires a certified consent management platform (CMP) integrated with the IAB Transparency and Consent Framework when serving personalized ads in the EEA and UK (since January 16, 2024) and in Switzerland (since July 31, 2024). For apps, the UMP SDK is Google's own option. The basic flow uses requestConsentInfoUpdate() to check the user's status, loadAndShowConsentFormIfRequired() to show the form when needed, and canRequestAds() before you load any ad. When the SDK reports that a privacy options entry point is required, add a button in settings that calls showPrivacyOptionsForm() so users can change their choice later.

Apps for Children: The Families Policy

If your target audience includes children, Google Play's Families policy adds strict rules. Ads shown to children or to users of unknown age must come only from Google Play Families Self-Certified Ads SDKs, must not use interest-based advertising or remarketing, and must be appropriate for children. When serving ads to those users, the policy prohibits, among other things:

  • Ads that take up the entire screen or interfere with normal use without a clear way to dismiss them.
  • Ads that interfere with normal use or game play, including rewarded or opt-in ads, that cannot be closed after 5 seconds.
  • Interstitial ads displayed immediately when the app launches.
  • More than one ad placement on a page.
  • Ads that are hard to tell apart from app content, such as offerwalls.
  • Shocking or emotionally manipulative tactics to encourage ad viewing or purchases, and dismiss buttons that trigger another ad.
  • Not distinguishing between virtual game coins and real money in purchases.

Google Play reserves the right to reject, remove or suspend apps for overly aggressive commercial tactics, so for a children's app, keep monetization deliberately light.

Google Play's Service Fee in 2026

Google Play's service fees are changing in 2026, and the rate now depends on where the user is:

  • Australia, the EEA, Japan, the UK and the US: a new structure applies from the regional rollout date (June 30, 2026 for the EEA, UK and US; September 30, 2026 for Australia and Japan). Auto-renewing subscriptions are charged 10% plus a 5% billing fee. For other transactions, the rate depends on whether the user first installed the app before or after the rollout date, on your annual earnings, and on whether you take part in programs such as Apps Experience or Play Games Level Up. The billing fee applies to purchases made with Google Play Billing and to the initial purchase of a paid app.
  • All other markets, until the new fees roll out globally: developers enrolled in the 15% service fee tier pay 15% on the first $1M (USD) they earn each year and 30% above that, and auto-renewing subscriptions are charged 15% regardless of earnings.

Metrics to Watch

AdMob's glossary defines the core ad metrics. The others are standard product metrics that you can calculate from your own analytics:

  • eCPM: estimated ad revenue per thousand impressions (earnings divided by impressions, times 1,000).
  • ARPDAU: average ad revenue per daily active user.
  • Match rate and show rate: how many ad requests got an ad back, and how many returned ads were actually displayed. Low values point to setup or timing problems.
  • Retention: the share of new users who come back after a given number of days. Aggressive ads or a paywall that appears too early often show up here first.
  • Lifetime value (LTV): the total revenue you expect from a user over the whole time they keep using the app. It matters most if you pay to acquire users.
  • Payer conversion: the share of users who make a purchase or start a subscription. Compare it against your own earlier releases rather than against published averages.

Read revenue and retention together. A change that raises ARPDAU but lowers retention can reduce total revenue over a few months, because fewer people stay to see the ads or buy the upgrade.

Key Takeaways

  • Pick the model that matches the value users get: ads, a one-time purchase, a subscription or a mix.
  • Digital goods, subscriptions and ad removal sold in the app must use Google Play's billing system unless you are enrolled in an eligible alternative program.
  • New apps and updates now need Play Billing Library 8 or newer.
  • Follow AdMob's interstitial and rewarded rules: natural breaks only, opt-in rewards, clear disclosure.
  • Ask for consent in the EEA, UK and Switzerland with a certified CMP such as Google's UMP SDK.
  • Apps for children must use Families self-certified ad SDKs and avoid disruptive formats.
  • Check the current service fee page for your users' regions, and watch revenue and retention together.
